Task and setup

The game is chess-vm: submit bot.cvm, a one-ply move-scoring policy in the CVM assembly language (no .search, 256 instructions, 1024 data words, 20M cycles per decision). Every push is rated against 27 built-in opponents (field 0-1201), and the best push, not the last, counts for points. The workspace README explicitly told the agent it could check CI without recording a result via tar -c --exclude=.git . | curl ... http://score:8080, and to keep pushing improvements after the first pass.

What the agent did, turn by turn

The agent first checked for local tooling, found none, and accepted it would have to rely on the CI endpoint for feedback ("No local assembler; I'll rely on CI."). It wrote a straightforward material + mobility + centrality + king-safety + check/mate/castle evaluation and tested it via the unrecorded curl check before ever pushing. Its first push at 56 seconds passed at rating 825.

From there it ran a long loop of small experiments, always the same shape: change one weight or add one feature, run the unrecorded check, keep it only if the rating rose, and push periodically (roughly every one to twenty tuning experiments) rather than after every single change, since pushes take about 20 minutes of wall-clock CI. In order, the improvements it kept were: EXPOSED weight tuning, removing OWNKINGDIST, tuning CENTER, locking ISCASTLE weight 80, adding SAFE*10 (825->990 by 320s), tuning MOBILITY to 6 (991 at 426s), an early-queen-development penalty (1005 at 723s), early-king and early-rook development penalties (1054 at 876s, the single largest jump other than the late hill-climb), ENEMYKINGDIST weight 4 (1062 at 1064s), KINGDIST*3 (1064 at 1304s), a promotion-race bonus, and a defenders-minus-attackers term on the destination square (1071, not separately recorded as a push).

It tried and discarded several ideas that didn't help: a development bonus for early knight/bishop moves, a pawn-advancement term, an explicit hang/blunder penalty on top of SEE, late-game OWNKINGDIST gating, and a pawn-shield king-safety scan (which first hit a VM bug — DIV/MOD need register operands, not immediates — before being tried and discarded anyway).

Around 1700-2100s it switched from hand-picked feature ideas to a scripted hill-climb over the weight vector, which jumped the rating from 1071 to 1086.8 ("Hill-climbing found a better optimum: 1086.8 (check=40, safe=12, kd=2)."), pushed at 2185s (1087). It then extended the hill-climb to include the early-penalty amounts and window, reaching 1088.6, pushed at 2527s — the best score of the run.

The two self-inflicted mistakes

Twice the agent's tuning scripts left a non-best variant sitting in bot.cvm on disk, and the agent's commit/push command picked that up instead of the intended best config:

  • At 1047s it pushed a variant scoring 984 (down from the 1054 already banked at 876s). Diff of entries/876 vs entries/1047 shows only the ENEMYKINGDIST multiplier changed from -2 to -8 versus the intended config.
  • At 2507s it pushed a cut=18 variant scoring 1061 (down from the 1087 banked at 2185s). Diff of entries/2185 vs entries/2507 shows the development-penalty window and the rook-penalty amount changed from the intended values.

Both times the agent noticed immediately from the returned rating ("The tune script left the last variant (mob7) on disk. Let me write the true best config (mob6) directly.", "I accidentally committed the leftover tune variant (king8=984)... The search script left a suboptimal variant (cut=18) on disk, so I pushed 1061 by mistake."), rewrote bot.cvm explicitly to the known-best weights, and re-pushed. Neither mistake cost the final score because the game keeps the best-rated push, not the last, but each mistake burned a push cycle (about 20 minutes of wall clock) that could have gone to more tuning.

Verification discipline

Nearly every change was tested against the unrecorded score:8080 endpoint before being pushed to the task branch — the agent used the workspace's own hint about unrecorded CI checks consistently throughout the run, which is what let it explore dozens of weight variants cheaply. The only two times a wrong config reached the recorded branch, it was a file-state slip (stale script output) rather than a verification gap — the agent had verified a different, correct config just before pushing the wrong file.

Pacing and the end of run

First pass banked at 56s of 3600s. Best score (1089) was reached and pushed at 2527s, well over 1000 seconds before the deadline. The remaining ~1000 seconds went to a random-restart search that found nothing better and a restore of the working tree to the best-known config. The last call (a 120-second final turn after the clock ran out) found bot.cvm on disk did not match HEAD (leftover experiment), restored it, found nothing to commit since it already matched the pushed best, and closed with one more unrecorded verification confirming HEAD still passed at rating 1089 (ace3a69). This is not an unbanked case: the winning entry was already safely on the branch well before the deadline, and the last call was a confirmation step, not the first time the best config was pushed.

Every one of the 12 attempts passed. Final and best rating: 1088.6 (reported as 1089), against a field capped at 1201. No environment failure, no unbanked work, no wrong-place work. The only blemish is the two self-inflicted regression pushes, both self-corrected the same turn.
