Why it passed anyway

chess-vm's verifier only fails a submission that faults or exhausts its cycle budget; any program that scores every legal move (even identically) passes and receives whatever rating the round robin gives it. The placeholder is exactly the baseline task.md warns about ("scores every move the same, so it plays at random"), and it happened to rate 316 against the 0–1201 field — enough to register as a pass, not because of anything the agent did.

Root cause

run.json's metrics corroborate the log evidence: "aborted_requests": 2, "requests": 9, and "request_seconds": 3596.652 against a limit_seconds of 3600 — essentially the entire wall-clock budget was spent inside two backend calls that never returned normally. proxy.error.log shows nothing indicative of a client-side problem beyond benign duplicate-header warnings from the upstream (Server: uvicorn vs Caddy), i.e. nothing that points to a malformed request from the agent. The stalls sit squarely on the llm.substrate.dev / openrouter/z-ai/glm-5.3 route the harness was configured to use.

Attribution

The agent did nothing wrong in the four seconds of real work it got: its file reads and orientation were sound and it had not yet had a chance to write or push anything before the backend swallowed its third call. Everything that decided the outcome — the 3474-second hang, the forced restart, the second 118-second hang, and the resulting empty-handed last-call push of the original placeholder — traces to the backend, not the agent's choices. This is an environment failure, not a reasoning or execution failure.
