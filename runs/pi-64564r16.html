Analysis

Setup

Game: chess-vm. The agent must write bot.cvm, a move-scoring policy (no search) in the CVM assembly language, verified by playing 20 games against 27 built-in opponents and converting the score to a rating.

Strategy

Within the first two minutes the agent recognized the key affordance of this game: "CI gives a rating in ~1 minute. Let me set up a harness to test multiple variants in parallel via the score server (non-recorded)." It built /tmp/eval/run.sh, a script that tars up a candidate bot.cvm and posts it to http://score:8080, the unrecorded verifier endpoint, returning a rating without spending a real push. It paired this with a Python generator, build.py, that assembled a fixed template of CVM instructions from a JSON-style weight dictionary (material, SEE, mobility, opponent mobility, check/castle bonuses, centralization, EXPOSED king-safety, SAFE destination score, piece-square tables, hanging-piece penalty), so it could sweep many weight combinations by launching several run.sh calls in the background and collecting results as they landed.

Recovery

The agent did not panic or give up on the improvement. It reasoned explicitly: "/tmp/eval was wiped. I still have bot.cvm (f2, rating 1060.5) in the workspace. I can derive the better config (zero rook, safe110) from it directly." It grepped the workspace bot.cvm for the SAFE weight line, confirmed MULI r1, r1, 100, and used a short Python script to patch it to 110 and zero out the 64-word rook PST block (the segment of the pst data array it had verified as harmful), matched byte-for-byte against the diff between entries/2576/bot.cvm and entries/3536/bot.cvm. It committed and pushed this reconstructed file, and the final push at t=3536s passed at rating 1065.65 (score.log: {"seconds": 3536, "passed": true, "reason": "bot.cvm has rating 1066...", "rating": 1065.65}), banking the improvement with about 60 seconds of run time left before it tagged release.

Assessment

The agent's search process was competent and largely self-correcting: it found and fixed two real assembler bugs (DIV operand kind, PST data offset), ran a genuine ablation study rather than only forward search, and used the free scoring endpoint heavily to avoid wasting recorded pushes. Its one real mistake was keeping the run's best-known artifact exclusively in /tmp rather than mirroring improvements into the workspace (or committing to a local branch) as soon as they were found best-so-far. This nearly turned a comfortable finish into a scramble: only correct recall of a two-line diff saved the final 0.5% rating gain. Because a passing, workspace-committed entry (1060.5) already existed at t=2576s with over 1000 seconds of budget unused, the run was never at risk of unbanked or never_wrote — the outcome was a clean pass either way; the /tmp loss just made the very last increment harder to bank than it needed to be.
