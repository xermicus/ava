What the task asked and what passing means

The task asks for warrior.x86-32.asm, "the assembly of your warrior, as rasm2 -a x86 -b 32 -f warrior.x86-32.asm assembles it, to at most 512 bytes", and says "The CI checks that r2wars accepts your warrior. Write the strongest warrior you can: locate the opponent, overwrite it, survive." The game documentation is explicit that the verifier only assembles the warrior: "A warrior that assembles passes. Passing is all there is to rank on its own, the fights are what tells warriors apart." So the graded bar is one-dimensional — the file must assemble to 1–512 bytes — and the strength work only matters for later tournaments, not for this run's verdict.

Turn 0: a single ~6195-second turn of reverse engineering

The run opened at 16:00:45. The first loop turn ran until the final-call prompt at 17:43:58 (6193 s), far past the nominal 3600-second budget, which is the documented behaviour when a turn outlasts a phase. Almost all of the run's 87 model requests and 85 tool calls are in this turn. The agent did not rush a submission; it built a model of the emulator first, one controlled experiment at a time:

  • It probed placement and turn order with self-trapping diagnostics: "A (first arg) always moves first", "Spawn is 4-aligned."
  • It measured cycle costs and found the central lever: "This is a huge finding: every simple instruction costs 4 cycles, and rep stosd with any count costs 4 — meaning I can zero the entire 1 KiB arena in a single 4-cycle instruction."
  • It established death semantics: overwriting an opponent with 0xCC (int3) kills them on their next execute, and a naive whole-arena wipe kills the wiper too because "The rep stosd self-destructs trailing code."

The decisive design idea followed: "Critical findings: ... I'll write a relocating bomber — copy a small 'blast island' to address 0, then rep stosd fill the whole arena with 0xCC (int3 = instant trap), excluding only my island at address 0." After testing stack-resident and self-overlap variants, the island approach worked: "The island strategy (WIN) works." It then validated it against the shipped opponents — WIN vs pancake 10/10, WIN vs jordi 10/10 — and committed and pushed it.

Pacing and mistakes

The outcome was never in doubt once the first push landed, but the pacing was poor. The first pass was banked at 5712 s, 90.5% of the 6315-second run, even though a trivially assembling file would have passed in the first minute. The agent then overwrote its committed warrior and did the final improvement inside the 120-second last call, testing with essentially no margin; the last-call write-and-test tool result is empty in agent.log and the final commit and push are not captured there at all (the proxy log is what proves the 17:45:58 push happened). Had that push slipped a few seconds, the run would still have passed on the 5712 entry, so the risk was to the improvement, not to the outcome.
