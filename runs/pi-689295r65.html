Task

The task (r2wars-x86-32) asks for warrior.x86-32.asm, a Core-War-style warrior for r2wars on radare2's ESIL emulator. The verifier only requires that the file assemble with rasm2 -a x86 -b 32 to between 1 and 512 bytes — passing is binary, and fight outcomes only affect ranking among tournament entries, not the pass/fail verdict.

23-200s: wrote a first 'bomber' warrior, hit an assembler quirk (equ forward references don't work in rasm2), fixed it, and had a 45-byte warrior that assembled cleanly. This was already a valid, passing submission by the game's actual rule (assembles, ≤512 bytes) — but it was never pushed.

200s-5279s: the agent shifted entirely into local fight-tuning, working out of /tmp rather than the workspace:

  • Iterated on step size (64→128→192→256→384→512) and copy length via generator scripts (genimp.py, fimp.py, makeimp.py), running dozens of batches of 30-100 fights each against jordi and pancake to compare win rates (e.g. "step-512 imp... really an oscillator between antipodal points", "step 128 and 192 look best (~74-75%)").

Why it nearly went wrong

The verifier's bar is low (valid assembly under 512 bytes) and was clearly met within the first few minutes. The agent instead used the entire run as an offline Core-War tuning session against two non-scoring example opponents, betting that it could always commit "later." That bet paid off only because the harness's forced turn transition happened to arrive with just enough runway left (~120s) for the agent to notice the wipe, recall its best variant, and push. Had the wipe landed even a couple of minutes later, or had the agent's recall of the exact working asm been less complete, this would have been a never_wrote or unbanked failure despite having had a correct answer since second 200.

Verdict

The engineering (ESIL mechanics investigation, imp+bomb design, empirical tuning) was competent and the final artifact is a reasonable warrior. But the run's outcome hinged entirely on agent time-management: it sat on a passing solution for over 5000 seconds while doing valuable-but-non-scoring optimization work outside the workspace, and was saved from likely failure only by the accident of still holding the winning code in context when its scratch space was wiped near the deadline.
