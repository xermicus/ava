What happened

The task (games/chess-vm) asks for bot.cvm, a one-ply move-scoring policy in CVM assembly, rated by playing 20 games against each of 27 built-in opponents (field 0-1201). The starter bot scores every move equally (random play, rating ~0-300 range).

The agent read README.md and SPEC.md, then noticed the loop-task instructions expose a CI oracle: tar -c --exclude=.git . | curl -s --data-binary @- http://score:8080 runs the identical scoring pipeline as a push, without recording an attempt. It exploited this immediately, writing exp.py, gen.py, search.py, and eval.py to generate candidate bot.cvm programs and score them via that endpoint.

The climb (11:07-11:58, ~50 minutes)

Progression of verified ratings, each confirmed against the live oracle:

  • Starter bot: rating 316 ("bot.cvm has rating 316", 11:07:33)
  • Hand-built MATERIAL+GAIN+SEE+MULI-8+MATEIN1+MOBILITY/OPPMOB: rating 917 (11:10:28)
  • Adding EXPOSED (-1) and OWNKINGDIST (-1): ~950s
  • Adding TORANK (+1): rating 969 (11:32)
  • Adding ATTACKERS on the destination square (-1): rating 989.6 (11:58:42), the eventual best

The agent reasoned correctly that MATERIAL (post-move material) and SEE (static exchange value) both include the immediate capture, so summing them double-counts it; MATERIAL - GAIN + SEE removes the double-count while keeping the exchange correction. It discovered the scorer serializes requests (~9s per verification regardless of worker count: 3 parallel workers took 30s, 6 took 63s, i.e. ~9-10.5s each), so it abandoned attempts at parallel search and did sequential single-feature screens instead.

The near-miss (12:01-12:05)

With the 989.6-rated config in hand and roughly 12 minutes of the 3600s budget left, the agent decided to attempt a bigger structural upgrade: piece-square tables (PSTs) stored in the CVM's 1024-word data section, indexed by destination/origin square with rank-mirroring for the side to move (via PLY parity). It wrote gen_pst.py with standard PST constants and a mirroring routine, but the generated assembly used MOD r3, r3, 2 — a literal 2 where the instruction set requires a register operand (an immediate form like MODI was needed). The oracle rejected it: "bot.cvm line 3: '2' is not a register, the registers are r0 through r15", surfaced at 12:01:28, with the deadline (started + 3600s) at 12:05:57 — under 4 and a half minutes left, most of which had already gone into writing and testing the broken generator.

The agent did not attempt to debug the PST generator. Its next assistant turn (12:04:37) states: "I need to submit right now... My best config was base + ATTACKERS -1: rating 989.6. Let me write that and commit/push immediately." It reconstructed the known-good bot.cvm from memory of the logged config, re-verified it via the oracle at 12:04:52 (rating 990 again), then committed and pushed at 12:04:56.

The push landed at second 3548 of the 3600-second budget — 52 seconds before the deadline, and the only push made in the entire run:

Judgment

The eval design work itself was sound and the final entry (rating 990, roughly 82% of the way from the weakest to the strongest opponent) is a solid result for a one-ply policy against a field that includes a four-ply searcher. But the run's pacing was reckless: the agent never banked a single passing push until the very last push of the run, holding the entire hour's worth of progress unrecorded and vulnerable. When it chose to spend its final minutes on a new, untested feature (PSTs) rather than banking the already-verified 990 config first, a routine syntax mistake very nearly cost the run its only pass — it was saved only because the broken experiment failed fast enough (in under a minute) and the agent had the discipline to abandon it immediately rather than debug under pressure. A run this close to unbanked is not meaningfully different from one that crosses into it; the correct move was to push the 990 config the moment it was found around 11:58, then continue experimenting with 40 minutes of margin.
