What happened

The task (chess-vm, single turn) asks for bot.cvm, a one-ply move-scoring policy in a small chess assembly language; the field is 27 built-in opponents rated 0 to 1201, and the deliverable is graded by pushing to the task git branch.

0:00-0:06 (11:06:00-11:11:55Z). The agent read README.md, task.md, SPEC.md and the stub bot.cvm, found no local engine, and discovered it could score a submission via a curl to the sandboxed score CGI endpoint without committing anything. It wrote a reasoned v1 policy — MATEIN1 short-circuit, MATERIAL - GAIN + SEE for exchange-aware material, MOBILITY/OPPMOB differential, CENTER, PROMO, a hanging-piece penalty gated on ATTACKERS/DEFENDERS, and an EXPOSED term — and scored it: rating 944, a large jump over the random-mover baseline of 316.

0:06-0:09. Timed a CI round trip at ~9 seconds, decided this was fast enough to tune against directly, and wrote tune/gen.py, a parametrized generator that reproduces the v1 bot from a weight dict.

0:09-0:45 (11:15-11:45Z). Roughly 30 minutes of manual grid search: one to three weights swept per call, each dispatched as a bash task, several run in the background and polled with sleep 60/180/240/280/290; tail .... Progress was real but slow — mob/oppmob/center reached 12/12/4 for rating 1006 by 11:29, then check/promo/hang/exposed were swept individually. Several polling calls came back empty because output was piped through tail inside a backgrounded shell, costing extra round trips to diagnose ("The output is empty because I used tail inside").

0:45 (11:45:11Z). Only at this point — three quarters of the budget gone — did the agent write tune/opt.py, an automated coordinate-descent optimizer, and launch it in the background with timeout 3000 (50 minutes), even though at most ~20 minutes of run budget remained. It then issued more sleep 280-290 polling calls to check on it, which is close to pure idle time from the run's perspective.

A second, identical verdict at 3560s (final harvesting of the branch state at run end) confirms the same entry. The run finished at 3576s of its 3600s budget.

Why it passed despite the risk

The agent treated the score dry-run endpoint as its only feedback loop and never once banked a commit as a fallback, even though it had a 944-rated working bot on disk six minutes in and a 1006-rated one twenty minutes later. Its margin for error was zero: had the optimizer's checkpoint file not survived the kill, or had the last-call turn started with less time or run into any friction (a slow git push, a flaky score endpoint), the run would have ended with nothing pushed to task at all, forfeiting a policy that was already well above the field's midpoint. The oversized timeout 3000 on the optimizer shows the agent lost track of its own remaining budget — it scheduled work for five times the time it actually had left instead of sizing the search to fit.

What was good

The scoring model itself was sound reasoning for a one-ply, no-search evaluator: resolving captures via SEE/GAIN before falling back to static material, weighting mobility over opponent mobility, and adding a hanging-piece term gated on local attacker/defender counts are all sensible for the CVM's constraints. Automating the search once it built tune/gen.py/tune/opt.py and having that optimizer persist best.json on every improvement was the one piece of defensive engineering that made the last-call recovery possible at all.
