Task

chess-vm asks for bot.cvm, a single-ply move-scoring policy in CVM assembly (no .search, capped at 256 instructions / 1024 data words / 20M cycles per decision). A push's rating comes from playing 20 games against each of 27 built-in opponents; score:8080 lets an agent test a submission against the same field without it counting as a graded push.

What the agent wrote

The policy first checks MATEIN1 and immediately scores a forced mate at 1,000,000, then otherwise combines a weighted sum of MATERIAL, SEE (exchange value), EXPOSED (hanging-piece penalty), MOBILITY - OPPMOB, GIVESCHECK, CENTER, ISCASTLE, PROMO, and ENEMYKINGDIST. This is a reasonable, idiomatic one-ply evaluation for the CVM's instruction set — it uses essentially every relevant chess-signal instruction the language exposes.

Iteration loop

The agent's approach was disciplined coordinate ascent:

  1. Get a first working version passing (it did, at 110s, rating 900.3), and push it immediately: "First test passed with a rating of 900.3 ... Let me commit and push this as the initial submission, then keep iterating." This banked a floor before any risk of running out of time.
  2. From there it tuned one weight at a time, re-testing via the score:8080 dry-run endpoint (28 such test posts logged in proxy.access.log) rather than burning graded pushes on exploratory changes. Example: "Now let's tune the SEE weight (currently 4)." → later "Now current best config (EXPOSED=14, SEE=8) → 928.0."
  3. It rolled back parameters that didn't help: "That change had no effect on the outcome (identical rating), so this weight isn't decisive here. Reverting to 20 and moving to the next parameter." and dropped an added SAFE-bonus term entirely ("No effect — removing to keep the program simple") rather than leaving dead weight in the program.
  4. It pushed again once it had a confirmed improvement (1149s, rating 937.4), and a third time after further tuning (3492s, rating 938.8), each push preceded by the exact score achieved locally so the push was not a gamble.

Outcome

All three graded pushes passed (score.log/run.json attempts all "passed": true), with rating monotonically increasing: 900.3 → 937.4 → 938.8, against a field spanning 0–1201. The final push landed at 3492s of a 3600s budget — 108 seconds of margin, tight but inside the window and recorded as a normal (non-last-call) passing attempt.

What could have gone better

The tuning was strictly one-parameter-at-a-time; a joint search (e.g. small randomized perturbations of the whole weight vector, or hill-climbing with occasional restarts) might have escaped the local optimum the agent converged to around rating ~939. The last push's 108-second margin to the deadline is also closer than ideal — a stall on any single dry-run test late in the run could have cost the improvement, though it did not.

Environment noise

Five requests to api.anthropic.com hung for the full 300s proxy timeout and were aborted ("status": 499), visible in proxy.access.log. These forced retries but did not affect the outcome, since the agent had already banked a passing solution 20+ minutes before most of them occurred and had budget to spare.
