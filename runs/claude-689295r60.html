The agent had to write bot.cvm, a one-ply move-scoring policy in the CVM assembly language, rated by playing 20 games against each of 27 built-in opponents (field ranges 0-1201, with a 4-ply searcher near the top).

Why the pushes passed

All 6 real pushes to the task branch passed verification; none failed. The agent's private syntax fault happened only against the free score endpoint and never reached a real push. Ratings recorded: 316 -> 928 -> 928 (repush, no change) -> 942 -> 942 (repush) -> 1008.

What went right

  • Correctly identified that score:8080 lets it iterate without cost, and used it disciplined: every real push was a value the agent had already confirmed would improve or match its recorded best. This is exactly the intended use of the two endpoints described in the AvA architecture.
  • Ablated aggressively and reverted immediately when an idea failed, keeping a clean, reproducible chain of checkpoints (v1, v3, v7, v16) rather than compounding untested changes.
  • Found the single highest-value feature (the hanging-piece safety scan) and correctly reasoned about why it worked ("penalizing our own blunders is reliable; 'rewarding' opponent hanging pieces misdetects and gets punished" — confirmed by testing the symmetric version, v19, which cratered to 717).

What went wrong / could have been better

  • A large share of the run's ~20 iterations (roughly a dozen) were spent on positional terms (mobility, center, king distance, development, EXPOSED/SAFE variants) that the agent's own commentary shows it expected to help but which consistently hurt. This suggests weak priors about which one-ply heuristics matter against searching opponents, and the safety-scan idea — clearly the most impactful — was only tried in the last 20 minutes of active iteration, well after most of the budget for that phase had already gone into a series of failed positional tweaks.
  • The agent stopped with roughly 3000 of 5400 budget-seconds unused, judging returns to be diminishing after only one round of refinement on top of the v16 breakthrough. Given that the safety scan alone was worth +66 rating points and came late, it is plausible (though not certain) that a comparably structured feature was still undiscovered; the agent's own text repeatedly cites cost-per-test (~10 min) as the reason to stop rather than a clear ceiling argument.
  • No environment or harness issues occurred; the single failure (the '1' is not a register bug) was self-inflicted but low-cost, caught immediately by the agent's own free-verification workflow.
