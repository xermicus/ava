What happened

The task (games/r2wars / r2wars-x86-32) asks for warrior.x86-32.asm: any warrior that assembles to 1-512 bytes passes the push; ranking against other seats happens later, in fights, and is not visible to the agent beyond its own local testing against the two bundled example warriors, jordi and pancake.

Turns 1-~14 (0-1150s): an expensive detour into reverse-engineering

The agent tried to read r2wars's own combat engine (a .NET binary) to learn exactly how deaths and cycle costs are computed, writing an ECMA-335 metadata parser and an IL disassembler from scratch. It hit repeated bugs ("Names are shifted — my row-size calc has a bug somewhere", "My US-string index mapping is wrong") and by 1140s explicitly recognized the detour was not paying off: "The decompilation rabbit hole is eating tokens—let me refocus on empirically building and testing the strongest warrior" and later "I'm burning too many tokens decompiling. Let me switch to pure empirical." Some of the RE findings (the 0xea byte being a universal invalid-opcode landmine, OOB reads/writes being fatal) were also reachable empirically and were in fact re-derived that way shortly after.

Turns ~14-29 (1150-1766s): building and banking v1/v2

At 1766s (33% of the 5400s budget) it committed and pushed this baseline, explicitly reasoning: "v2 passes CI (accepted). It's a recorded passing baseline... let me commit and push this as a safety net, then continue optimizing." The push succeeded (remote: pipeline: {"seconds": 1766, "passed": true}), matching score.log's first attempt.

The agent kept iterating: a "mobile core" family (v3, v5, v6, v7) that relocates its live code each cycle repeatedly broke (v5: 0/20 vs jordi, traced through multiple failed ESIL step-traces to a copy/jump bug never fully fixed), so it reverted to the v2 poison-cloud and instead swept its footprint/gap/floor parameters. Net result

Both pushes pass the game's only pass/fail bar (assembles to a legal size), so the run is a pass. But the entry that will actually fight in tournaments is the weak 18/30-vs-pancake baseline, not the 79/100-vs-pancake warrior the agent spent two-thirds of its budget validating. The gap between "agent's best known solution" and "solution on the branch" is the whole story of this run: good empirical methodology, a well-timed early bank, and then a persistent failure to close the loop on its own improvements.
