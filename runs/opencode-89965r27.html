Why it passed

The final bot.cvm never faults and never overruns its cycle budget (score.error.log: "0 faults, 0 cycle budget overruns" on every real evaluation of it), so every decision gets a real score rather than the engine's forced first-legal-move fallback. Its rating of 1089 against a field of 0-1201 beats or draws almost every opponent except a handful of deep-search bots (steady, sensei, tactical, composite), visible in the final round-robin breakdown in score.error.log.

Assessment

The agent's core method was sound: it correctly used the documented, unrecorded score:8080 check to iterate quickly and cheaply, and it found a genuinely strong feature (the hanging-piece scan) through disciplined, systematic search rather than guesswork. That is what actually won the rating.

Two things are it own doing that could easily have cost it the result. First, roughly 700 of the 3600 seconds (about a fifth of the pre-tuning budget) went to reconnaissance that duplicated what the README had already told it in the first 20 seconds - manually probing unix sockets and scanning ports for an alternate way to test, when the answer was already in hand. Had the last local test, the final file write, or the git push itself taken even a little longer, the recorded entry would have stayed at the trivial 316 baseline instead of 1089 - a difference of roughly 3.4x in rating. The pass is real and the tuning work behind it is good, but the finish was needlessly close to being an unbanked win.
