Chess-VM run analysis

What happened

The agent read README.md, task.md, SPEC.md, and the starter bot.cvm, then went straight to checking whether it could get feedback faster than a git push:

"Let me look at the git state and try the scoring endpoint with the baseline first."

It ran tar -c --exclude=.git . | curl ... http://score:8080 directly against the scoring sidecar and got a baseline rating of 316 in about 1.6 seconds — much faster than a full push/verify cycle. This became the foundation of the whole run.

First real policy (pushed at 333s)

The agent wrote a policy combining SEE-style capture evaluation, a hanging-piece scan, piece-square tables, mobility, and an endgame king-driving term. Scored locally it jumped to 1145 (baseline 1201 is the top of the field, a four-ply search opponent):

"316 → 1145 (top of field is 1201). Let me commit and push this, then tune."

It committed and pushed to the task branch immediately, banking a passing entry (score.log seconds=333, rating 1145.25) very early — about 9 minutes into a 3600-second budget. This is the key risk-management move: a valid, decent entry was on the branch well before any further work, so nothing downstream could turn this into a never_wrote or unbanked failure.

Building a tuning harness

With a fast, deterministic local scorer available, the agent built:

  • gen.py: renders bot.cvm from a weight dictionary (PST tables, aggregation modes, term weights).
  • run.py: tars just bot.cvm, POSTs it to the score endpoint, caches results by content hash.
  • Noise measurement: it perturbed unrelated parameters and found "noise is ~±10 ELO, but the metric is deterministic, so I can optimize it directly."
  • lab.py: jitter-averaged evaluation, since it initially found "the jitters were inert" and had to patch a real tie-break jitter table into gen.py to get real variance (σ≈28) before trusting the search.
  • cd.py: coordinate descent over categorical/numeric weight axes (hang_agg, held_agg, threat_agg, mob_w, castle_w, gain_w, pst_pct, etc.), run with a ThreadPoolExecutor to parallelize evaluations (it checked nproc and tuned worker count for throughput).

It ran two long coordinate-descent passes in the background (launched with 1800s timeouts) while continuing to extend the generator with king-safety and trade terms, checking on progress via the background task's output file rather than blocking.

Second push (banked at 2988s)

The coordinate descent produced a large jump ("Big jump: mean 1157 → 1206"). The agent verified the winning parameter set with a fresh run.py call, regenerated bot.cvm from it, and pushed:

"1218 — above the top of the field. Pushing this."

score.log seconds=2988, rating 1218.28 — passed, and above the strongest opponent in the field (max 1201). The agent then confirmed git status --porcelain was clean and task was already at the pushed commit, and stopped there with roughly 600 seconds of the 3600s budget unused.

Verification discipline

Every claim was checked against the real score endpoint before being acted on: the baseline was measured, not assumed; noise was quantified before trusting small deltas; the jitter mechanism was debugged when it produced no variance instead of being trusted blindly; and the final tuned parameters were re-verified with a clean run.py call before being written into the pushed bot.cvm and committed. No claim in the final summary is unverified against score.log.

Environment

proxy.access.log shows one aborted (status: 499) POST to api.anthropic.com at 23:03:18 — essentially at the run's own finish time (23:03:37) and after the final result event (stop_reason: end_turn, is_error: false) had already been produced. This reads as a request cut short by the harness winding down after the agent's own natural stop, not a mid-task backend failure, and it had no bearing on the outcome: both pushes were already recorded in score.log well before this point.

Verdict

Both pushes passed (score.log: 333s rating 1145, 2988s rating 1218), well clear of the 3600s budget. This is a clean success driven entirely by the agent's own initiative — discovering the local scoring shortcut, quantifying and correcting for measurement noise, and running a real optimization loop rather than guessing weights by hand.
