What happened

The task (games/chess-vm) asks for bot.cvm, a single-decision move-scoring policy in the CVM assembly language, played against a fixed field of 27 built-in opponents rated 0-1201 by a seeded round robin. A submission cannot search; it scores each legal move once and the engine plays the top score.

Reading and orientation (L0-L90)

The agent read README.md, task/SPEC.md, and the starter bot.cvm, then benchmarked its own iteration loop: time (tar -c --exclude=.git . | curl ... http://score:8080), confirming that a full local CI verification takes about 1.6s. This is the correct move for this game — the book warns the round robin takes minutes but a single submission's verification takes seconds, and the agent found this itself before committing to a workflow.

First policy and first push (L98-L234)

Baseline (an essentially-random bot) rated 316. The agent wrote gen.py, a Python generator that emits CVM assembly from a weight dict — SEE-based material, piece-square tables, a threat sweep, king-ring pressure, mobility — worked around a CVM constraint ("DIV/MOD need register operands, not immediates" — L211) by baking percentages into data tables instead, and got a first working policy to 1117 on the first real submission. It correctly treated this as a save point: "1117 already — near the top of the field. Let me commit this as a first passing submission, then tune." (L225) and pushed to git, banking a pass at 699s.

Tuning to the top of the field (L235-L338)

It wrote tune.py, a coordinate-descent hill-climber that calls the CI score endpoint directly per candidate weight set. It found and fixed a bug in its own filter logic (cand[k]==base[k] or cand[k]<0 incorrectly rejected valid negative-weight candidates) before trusting the tuner's output. The tuner converged at rating 1229 — above 1201, the rating of the strongest opponent in the field — and the agent pushed this at 1956s: "Tuner converged at 1229 (above the strongest opponent's rating). Let me lock that in." This is effectively the ceiling the game offers: beyond out-rating every fixed opponent, the metric cannot meaningfully separate the submission further under this scoring rule.

Diminishing-returns phase (L377-L683)

With more than 40% of the 3600s budget left and the field topped, the agent switched to building a from-scratch local chess engine plus CVM interpreter (dev/engine.c) to get a faster, noise-free test loop than the live CI ("the CI signal is noisy (±60 Elo swings from 1-cp weight changes)"). This is defensible in principle but turned into a large, only partially productive side project:

  • The assembler section of engine.c "got tangled" and had to be cut and rewritten (L385-L408).
  • Move generation was validated against a perft suite (good discipline) before trusting the engine for match play.
  • The local lab found a genuine problem — 66 of 100 games between the bot and a local opponent ended in repetition draws, caused by the bot's own move oscillation — and fixed it locally (0.7575 to 0.8325 win rate).
  • That local improvement did not reproduce on the real CI (1203 vs the already-banked 1229): "Local improvement didn't transfer to CI ... within CI noise."
  • The agent tried several more structural ideas (w_chase, w_corner, w_ekd) via an experiment runner and found nothing that beat 1229.

It correctly refused to overwrite the known-good bot.cvm with any of this unverified local work: "bot.cvm is still the best-rated (1229) version — that entry is safe." (L682). The final action, at 3502s during the harness's last call, committed only the dev tooling (engine.c, lab.py, exp.py, updated .gitignore) and pushed — bot.cvm unchanged, verified again by the pipeline at rating 1229.

Why it passed

The pass was secured at 1956s, 54% into the budget, with a rating that exceeds the entire field — there was no dependency on the last call, and no risk of an unbanked or regressed final state, because the agent used the sandbox score endpoint to gate every git push and declined every local-only result it couldn't confirm against the real verifier.

What could have been better

The last ~1650s of budget produced no measurable improvement. Building a bespoke engine + interpreter to fight CI noise is a reasonable idea once diminishing returns set in, but the field was already beaten at the point the agent chose to start it; time there was traded for confidence and tooling, not for score. This is not a mistake that endangered the outcome, but it is the clearest inefficiency in an otherwise clean run: the agent kept investigating after the metric it was optimizing had nothing more to give.
