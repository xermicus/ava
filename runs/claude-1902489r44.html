Why it passed

The run passed cleanly, and it passed twice over: once at t=1514s with a modest but valid 799-rated policy, and again at t=3552s with a stronger 948-rated one. The first push is what actually decided the outcome. The agent chose to bank a finished, if unpolished, solution well before spending the bulk of its budget on tuning and restructuring — a sound insurance move given that CVM tuning against a hill-climber has no guaranteed stopping point. When the second, better attempt came together with only 48 seconds of the 3600s budget left, a slower final round (say, one more minute of tuning, or a slower generate-and-verify step) would have cost it nothing but the improvement: the 799 entry would still have stood as the run's entry of record. The margin looks alarming in isolation, but the run's actual risk profile was low because of the earlier bank.

What could have gone better

The stated "~25 min" push-latency belief was simply false (both real pushes cleared verification in under 5 seconds) and, while harmless here, reflects a guess presented as fact rather than something the agent checked directly by timing its own first push (it had that data immediately after the 11:42:24 push and could have corrected the belief). Getting this right would likely have let the agent do a cheap real push after the first tuning round (~921 rating) as a second insurance point, before committing to the riskier structural rewrite that temporarily dropped the local score to 832. As it stands the strategy worked, but it worked with less margin than it needed to.
