What the agent did

The agent treated r2wars as a reverse-engineering plus empirical-search problem and worked the entire budget on it. It started by reading the task and the r2wars docs, then decompiled the engine (strings/monodis attempts on r2wars.dll) and probed it empirically: it measured that every instruction costs 4 cycles ("All instructions cost 4 cycles (uniform in this engine)") and identified that death comes from executing a trap (int3/ud2) or accessing outside the arena. It built a reusable fight harness (/tmp/fight.sh, then /tmp/one.sh and /tmp/tourney.sh) that plays a candidate against an opponent over N rounds and tallies wins/losses/draws, and a pool of opponents drawn from the provided examples (jordi, pancake) plus its own designs.

It then iterated through a series of warrior designs, each motivated by the previous one's failure mode: an imp ("The imp works"), a forward-marching replicator that "runs off the arena end", a wrap-around replicator w2, a robust byte-wise wrap replicator w3 that "beats both examples", a clean bomber cb, a trail bomber tb, a scorched-earth full bomber c3 (which self-destructs), and finally a fixed-core sweeper (sweep2/fs) that bombards the whole arena with 0xcccccccc (int3 traps) while protecting the 80 bytes around its own core. The two designs that mattered at the end were w3 and the sweeper.

Why the pushes passed

The verifier for r2wars-x86-32 only checks that the warrior assembles with rasm2 to 1–512 bytes; combat strength is decided later by tournament fights, not by the per-push verifier. Both submitted warriors were valid assembly, so both pushes passed. The agent discovered this late: after the tar | curl http://score:8080 test at 15:45:26 returned a pass, it reasoned "The CI just checks that r2wars accepts the warrior ... passed just means the warrior assembles and is valid", and from then on treated grading as validation-only rather than strength.

Pacing and the decisive moment

The budget went almost entirely into design and testing: from 15:07 to 16:05 (~58 of the 60 minutes). It had a verifier-confirmed passing warrior as early as 15:45:26 (elapsed ~2280s) via the unrecorded tar | curl test, yet it did not push to the branch until 16:05:12 (elapsed 3515s), committing w3 and banking the first recorded pass with only ~85 seconds of the 3600s budget to spare. That late bank was the decisive act: had the first push not landed before the cut, the run would have depended entirely on the last call.

In the last call (which began at elapsed ~3540s, per the monitor {"elapsed_seconds":3540,"last_call":true,"turn":2}), the agent second-guessed its choice. Its closing thoughts show it weighing the two: "sweep2/fs (fixed-core sweepers) survive forever but are slower to corrupt, c3 ... fast but eventually self-destructs, w3 is a fast replicator that beats sweepers". It then overwrote the workspace warrior with the sweeper and pushed it at 16:06:55 (elapsed 3618s); that push also passed, so the entry of record is the sweeper. Its last recorded action (16:06:16) was still re-testing the sweeper against a passive sleeper because it had "showed a self-destruction risk (lost a round to a passive sleeper), which is a red flag — an unkillable warrior must NEVER die to a passive opponent" — but by then the banked w3 pass at 3515s had already secured the outcome, so the risk was confined to which entry ranked.

What went wrong

The main agent-side fault is pacing: a verifier-confirmed pass sat from ~2280s until 3515s before it was pushed to the branch, leaving almost no margin and forcing the outcome to hinge on the last call. The last-call swap from the proven w3 to the sweeper it had just seen self-destruct was a gamble that only paid off because the verifier checks assembly validity, not strength. Nothing in the environment failed: the backend, harness, sandbox and verifier all worked, both pushes were graded, and the only sidecar noise (nginx duplicate-header warnings, one aborted request) was harmless.
