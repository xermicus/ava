Why the pushes passed or failed

The two failing pushes were pure CVM syntax mistakes introduced while writing the very first draft under acute time pressure — not conceptual bugs in the scoring policy. Both were exactly diagnosed by the verifier's one-line reason (SPEC.md compliance: LD's third operand is an immediate, JLE needs ra, rb, label) and fixed with single, correct edits on the first try. The three passing pushes reflect a genuinely reasonable one-ply evaluation: material via MATERIAL/SEE, destination-square safety via ATTACKERS/DEFENDERS, mobility, king safety, castling and development heuristics, tuned incrementally — this is why the rating climbed from 880 to 884 across the last two iterations rather than regressing.

What went right

Given only ~115 seconds of real slack after the hang resolved, the agent's execution was tight: it diagnosed and fixed two independent one-line syntax errors from terse verifier messages without wasted turns, and it used every remaining second productively, banking a pass and then two incremental improvements instead of freezing or over-editing.

What went wrong

The core issue is external: a single LLM completion (documented in proxy.access.log) stalled for 3446 of the run's 3600 seconds — a x100 slowdown versus comparable requests before and after it — which is a backend/gateway fault, not something the agent did. But the agent's own strategy compounded the exposure to that risk: it chose to fully deliberate on the scoring formula (twice, across an aborted 15.7K-character thinking block and a resumed 21.4K-character one) before writing anything to bot.cvm, despite having explicitly noted it had no way to test locally. A cheaper, safer opening move — write and push a trivial-but-correct scorer immediately, then iterate — would have banked a low-but-valid rating within the first minute and made the run's outcome independent of exactly when in the hour the backend happened to stall. As it was, the run's success depended entirely on the hang resolving with just enough time left to write, debug and push once.

Bottom line

The run passed (final recorded rating 884, comfortably mid-field of 0-1201), but it passed by a margin measured in tens of seconds against a 3600-second budget, and that margin existed only because a catastrophic backend stall happened to end just in time. The decisive event was environmental; the near-total absence of safety margin around it was the agent's own doing.
