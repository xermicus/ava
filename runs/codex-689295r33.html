What the run was

r2wars-x86-32 asks for warrior.x86-32.asm, and the verifier only requires it to assemble (with rasm2) to 1-512 bytes. Passing is therefore trivial: any valid warrior passes. The fights that distinguish warriors are not part of the single-run verdict. The run passed: two graded pushes, at 1685s and 3498s, both {"passed": true}.

Turn 1 (main run, 0-~3600s)

Why the pushes passed

Both passed simply because each is a valid 16-28 byte x86-32 warrior that rasm2 assembles, which is the entire verifier. No warrior's quality enters the verdict. The second half of the budget — the fight-testing, parameter tuning, and sub-agent parallelization — was spent improving win rates against the two example opponents, a metric the single-run verifier does not use.

Assessment

The agent did solid reverse-engineering of the game mechanics and verified its work properly (assembling, probing the score endpoint, and confirming push verdicts), and it banked a passing push early, so the run is a clean pass. Its real error was scope: it treated a trivial pass-on-assembly task as a competitive warrior-building exercise and burned roughly half the budget optimizing a criterion the verifier ignores. Its own numbers show the final submission (~90% vs examples) was barely better than the early baseline (~88%), so even the optimization it pursued did not move the needle. The run's outcome was decided at 1685s, and the rest was wasted effort that a leaner agent would have spent confirming and stopping.
