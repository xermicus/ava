What happened

The task was to write warrior.x86-32.asm, a warrior for r2wars that the verifier only requires to assemble to between 1 and 512 bytes. The agent aimed well above the minimum and worked to produce a genuinely strong fighter.

Turn 0 (start to 1843s): reconnaissance and design

The agent opened by studying the game rather than guessing. It fought the two shipped x86-32 examples against each other (r2wars --fight ... jordi ... pancake, winner jordi after 160/341 cycles), located the install and its examples, and inspected the .NET DLL with strings to see the internal machinery (GetCycles, MAX_CYCLES, GetMemAccessRead/Write).

It then established which bytes are lethal to execute, building tiny warriors and fighting them against pancake:

round 1: pancake.x86-32 wins after 3 cycles, int3.x86-32 died: Syscall/Int Instruction executed
round 2: pancake.x86-32 wins after 4 cycles, hlt.x86-32 died: ESIL trap

This confirmed int3, hlt and ud2 reliably kill whoever runs them, giving it a bomb payload (0xCC / int3).

The first real detour was its fight harness. It wrote fight.sh to tally win rates, but the first version compared full file paths while r2wars prints basenames without the .asm:

sweeper.x86-32.asm wins=0 | jordi.x86-32.asm wins=0 | draw=30 (n=30)

even though a direct run showed winner: sweeper.x86-32. The agent debugged this (used cat -A to see the line, confirmed the .asm omission) and fixed the harness to strip the extension, after which numbers became meaningful:

sweeper.x86-32 wins=30 | jordi.x86-32 wins=0 | draw=0 (n=30)
sweeper.x86-32 wins=13 | pancake.x86-32 wins=17 | draw=0 (n=30)

It iterated through designs: a byte-granular sweeper (loses the race to pancake), a dword-granular sweeper (15/30 vs pancake), then a marching bomb-dropper that drops a dense 0xCC trail and copies itself forward by 128 bytes. This marcher beat pancake 18/30, then, after fixing the copy length from 68 to 72 to cover the full 69-byte block, 29/40 and 45/60, and beat jordi 30/0. A dword-bombing marcher was worse (23/40), and head-to-head the byte marcher crushed it 38/2.

Turn 2 (1843s to ~3481s): further exploration, no overwrite

After banking the pass the agent kept experimenting: a marching sweeper (msweep, 25/40 vs pancake but 17/23 against the banked marcher), a compact STEP=64 marcher (34/40 vs pancake, 30/0 vs jordi — but then 0/40 vs its own STEP=128 baseline), a pure imp replicator (marcher beat it 30/0), and the marching-sweeper vs sweeper4 (that command's output is not shown before the run's tail).

The STEP=64 result was decisive evidence: despite winning against the examples, it self-corrupted because the copy overlapped live code, losing 0/40 to the STEP=128 marcher. The agent recognized this as a fatal robustness flaw and discarded it. It also checked for edge-of-arena out-of-bounds reads by running a detailed fight and confirming the marcher only ever died to pancake's bombs, not to its own addressing.
