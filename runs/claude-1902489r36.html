Task

Write bot.cvm, a one-ply move-scoring policy in the CVM assembly language, scored by rating against 20 games each versus 27 built-in opponents (field 0-1201).

What the agent did

After reading README.md, task.md and the full SPEC.md (the workspace started with a trivial bot.cvm that scores every move 0, i.e. plays randomly), the agent built a first heuristic combining material, mobility, checks, castling and similar terms, and pushed it. It passed at 932, its own quoted first CI result:

"931 — passing, field is 0..1201. Now improving. Two big missing ideas: threat detection ... and passed pawn detection, plus an EXPOSED penalty."

From there it ran a tight loop for the rest of the run: add or change exactly one term, push, read the rating, and decide keep/revert. The trace of its own commentary shows this cleanly:

  • Threat-detection loop: "the JLE r7, r0, L_thrnext / JMP L_thrnext pair means enemy pieces are never actually scored (both paths jump away)" — 911 vs 931 baseline, reverted.
  • Passed-pawn scanner: "right-side blockers jump back to the loop start instead of exiting, and the scan starts from the from-square rather than the to-square" — rewrote with TOSQ, measured 881 (worse), diagnosed as "my passer test ignores enemy pawns beside/behind the destination", dropped the term entirely, confirmed back to 931.
  • King-pressure term: caught its own ATTACKERS/DEFENDERS semantic confusion ("for the enemy king square that's their own defenders"), fixed it, still measured worse (908), reverted.
  • Capture-tempo tuning found +1 point (932, new best) — committed and pushed as the first recorded improvement over the initial 931/932 push.
  • Mobility weight sweep (×4 → ×2 → ×1 → ×3) found ×2 as a clear local optimum, jumping the rating from 932 to 962 in one change — its biggest single lever.
  • Check bonus, castle bonus (60→80→100, converged at 80), knight-centrality (5→6→7, converged at 6) were each bracketed with two or three test points either side of the current value.
  • A development-bonus bug ("LD r2, r5, 0 uses r5 directly as index without the base" — indexing into the wrong data table) was caught from a bad rating (890 vs 971), fixed, found rating-neutral, and removed again to keep the program lean rather than kept for no benefit.
  • A late "hanging-enemy-piece" detector was tried with inverted DEFENDERS/ATTACKERS logic (839), the inversion diagnosed and fixed (972, still below the 987 baseline), and abandoned as not earning its cycle cost.
  • A destination-risk term (using ATTACKERS on the post-move square) was the second-biggest single win, taking the rating from 972 to 982, then 984 after retuning its divisor, then 988 after capping the multiplier at 2.

The agent tracked a running best throughout and pushed it six times as it improved: 932 (1056s) → 971 (1151s) → 972 (1193s) → 982 (1218s) → 984 (1247s) → 987 (1385s) → 988 (1656s), all confirmed by score.log/run.json attempts, all passing.

Why it worked

The agent treated every change as a hypothesis to be falsified against the real scorer rather than something to reason its way into keeping. This shows up as a consistent pattern across roughly 30 logged iterations: propose an idea grounded in chess or CVM semantics, implement it, measure the CI rating, and only keep it if the number went up. When a change regressed, the agent diagnosed the actual instruction-level bug (register misuse, inverted comparisons, wrong base address) rather than shrugging and moving on, which let it distinguish "this idea is bad" from "this idea was buggy" and sometimes retry a corrected version.

Critically, none of the several bugs introduced during tuning (threat scan, passed pawns, development table, hanging-piece detector) ever reached a pushed submission in its broken form; the agent always re-measured before recording a new best. The final push (988, at 1656s of a 3600s budget) reflects a clean, converged policy: "the tuning is converging — most single-weight changes now land within ±9 and the best is a plateau at 987-988," after which it stopped and tagged release per the task's own instructions rather than continuing to spend budget for noise-level gains.

Nothing in monitor.json, proxy.error.log, or score.error.log indicates any environment-caused disruption; scoring ran normally on every one of the seven pushes.
