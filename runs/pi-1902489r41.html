What the agent did

The task (chess-vm) asks for bot.cvm, a move-scoring policy written in a restricted CVM assembly language with no search, data, or stack beyond what SPEC.md allows: a submission scores one move at a time and the engine plays whichever move the program scores highest. The agent read README.md, task.md, and SPEC.md, then checked the sandbox for any existing chess tooling (which cvm assembler python3 gcc, python3 -c "import chess") and found none.

Rather than writing CVM by hand and hoping it was correct, it built its own verification stack first:

  • A compact Python chess engine (tools/chess_engine.py), validated against known perft counts (move-generation counts by depth) to catch bugs in castling, en passant, and promotion handling. It iterated on this several times, e.g. fixing a KING_OFF offset list that was missing an offset, and an en-passant capture bug found via a synthetic KRK-type position.
  • A CVM assembler/interpreter (tools/cvm.py) reimplementing SPEC.md's instruction set and chess-specific opcodes (PIECEAT, SEE, MATEIN1, OPPMOB, GIVESCHECK, MOBILITY, EXPOSED, ATTACKERS, KINGDIST, etc.) on top of the Python engine, so it could run bot.cvm locally exactly as the real scorer would.
  • A match runner (tools/play.py) to pit its bot against baseline opponents (random, simple heuristics) over batches of games.

The policy itself

bot.cvm evaluates the position after a candidate move using full piece-square tables (material baked in, mirrored for the moving side, with separate midgame/endgame king tables gated on a non-pawn piece count ≤ 4), corrected by SEE on the destination square to account for recaptures a static eval can't see. On top of that it layers: forced mate detection (MATEIN1 scored at 1,000,000), stalemate handling (avoid it when winning, seek it when losing), a check bonus, a castling bonus, a queen-promotion bonus, a mobility-difference term, an "exposed valuables" penalty, and (added in the second iteration) a king-danger term counting enemy attackers around the king's neighbor squares, an endgame king-approach bonus once clearly winning, and a penalty for early queen moves before other pieces have developed.

Timeline and pacing

The agent pushed its first working version at 2620s of the 3600s budget (73% through) — commit message "CVM chess policy: PST eval + SEE + mate/stalemate handling." This passed and was rated 961 (field 0–1201). Rather than stopping, it kept using the remaining ~900s to run more self-play batches, found the first version's mobility weighting and lack of king safety left it weaker in some lines, and iterated:

Why it passed

The passing threshold for this game is just "the verifier reaches a verdict"; the rating itself (0–1201 field) is what standings are built from. The agent's early bank at 2620s guaranteed it would keep at least the 961-rated entry regardless of what happened afterward — pushing is free and a failed push costs nothing, and it used that fact correctly. The subsequent, better push benefited from real self-testing (perft-correct move generation, a spec-accurate interpreter) rather than guesswork, which is why the tuning it applied late (king safety, endgame approach) translated into a real rating gain (961 → 975) instead of a regression.

Where it fell short

The agent spent a disproportionate share of its 3600s budget re-deriving infrastructure (a full chess engine, plus a matching CVM interpreter) instead of directly reasoning about bot.cvm against SPEC.md — a reasonable choice given no chess libraries were available in the sandbox, but it introduced its own bug surface (the missing KING_OFF entry, the en-passant handling bug, the SUBI typo) that had to be found and fixed before its self-testing became trustworthy. Its final go/no-go decision between the 961-safe version and the 975 gamble was made on genuinely weak evidence (10-game samples, a self-described "flawed" KRK test), and it got lucky that the riskier choice paid off; nothing in the transcript shows it computing a rigorous confidence interval before making that call. Neither of these affected the pass/fail outcome, since a valid entry was banked well before either push.
