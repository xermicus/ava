What happened

This is a chess-vm run: the agent must write bot.cvm, a one-ply move-scoring policy in the CVM assembly language, and the README frames it as a 'loop task' — push as soon as something passes, then keep improving, since only the best-rated push (not the last) counts.

Setup and first pass (0-1211s). The agent read README.md, task.md and SPEC.md, then discovered the starter bot.cvm (ldi r0,0; score r0; halt) and confirmed the local CI mirror is fast (tar -c --exclude=.git . | curl ... http://score:8080, ~2-4s per call). It scored the starter (316) and immediately wrote gen.py, a Python generator that emits bot.cvm from a dict of tunable weights, encoding material+SEE, a hanging-piece scan, piece-square tables, mobility and an endgame drive. That first real policy scored 1052 and was committed and pushed at 1211s, the run's first passing entry (entries/1211/bot.cvm, score.log line 1).

v2 and the coordinate-descent win (1211-1887s). The agent added pawn-attack detection and an enemy-threat term, scoring 1143 locally, pushed it at 1747s (entries/1747). It then built tune.py, a coordinate-descent tuner that scores parameter variants against the local CI in parallel via a thread pool, backed by a JSON result cache keyed by a hash of the generated source (so repeats never re-score). A grid sweep over hangnum, thrnum, oppmob, mymob, check, castle, devmove, qearly lifted the rating to 1226, quoted directly in the transcript: hangnum= 1 1122.7 / hangnum= 2 1151.6 <== / hangnum= 4 1139.0 etc., ending at base 1151.... The agent regenerated bot.cvm from tune/best.json and pushed at 1887s — bot.cvm has rating 1226, the field ranges from 0 to 1201 (entries/1887). This is the decisive push: 1226 is above the field's strongest opponent (1201), and it is the score the run's four attempts top out at.

The unproductive back half (1887-3608s). From here the agent tried a long sequence of additional ideas, none of which beat 1226:

  • A king-safety term keyed to a lookup table needing a new register — abandoned quickly ("the (useless) king-ring term" removed later).
  • Tapered PeSTO-style piece-square tables interpolated by game phase — tested pesto vs simple at several taper ratios, all worse than 1226 (best 1197.8), with the agent's own diagnosis: "The taper hurt — my phase includes pawns, so the king centralizes too early."
  • A piece-only phase accumulator (fixing the above) — still worse (1202.8 best case, simple 1 1 at 1202.8).
  • tune2.py, a multi-pass coordinate descent with a 12-point acceptance margin (to absorb rating jitter) — reran the grid over phlo/phspan and other params, best result 1220.9, still short of 1226.
  • A trade-when-ahead scaling term — explicitly rejected: "Trade term hurts — leaving it off."
  • A mirrored pawn-attack term (enemy pieces attacked by our pawns) — every variant tested was worse than the 0 baseline (pawnthr 0 1220.9 vs pawnthr 1 1197.8 and worse).
  • A background randomized hill-climber (hc.py, run via nohup ... &, budgeted 2700s) — after ~4 minutes of sampling it had found nothing: [67] 1220.9 (was 1220.9) {'egphase': (700...}, i.e. every mutation it tried was a no-op or a loss, and the agent killed it (pkill -f hc.py).
  • A final "risk of losing an exchange" heuristic — every parameter combination tried scored exactly the same 1220.9 as the base, meaning the term had no effect at all in the tested range.

At the end ("Time's up — restoring the best-scoring bot.cvm (the 1226 version already on task)") the agent ran git checkout -- bot.cvm to discard its in-progress (unproven) edits and restore the known-good 1226 file, verified by diffing the byte content: entries/1887/bot.cvm and entries/3608/bot.cvm are identical (same md5). The 3608s push (the run's last call, monitor.json shows last_call: true at 3541s elapsed) therefore just re-submitted the same policy alongside pushing its tooling files — a no-op safety push, not new work.

Why it passed

The game only requires producing a valid bot.cvm that scores every legal move without faulting; the agent did this on its very first real generated policy (1052) and improved twice more through legitimate, CI-verified gains, ending comfortably above the top opponent's rating. Every one of the four pushes reported passed: true in score.log.

What could have gone better

The grid search that produced the 1226 jump (multiple weights swept together) worked; the failed post-1226 attempts were mostly evaluated one parameter or one idea at a time rather than folded into the same systematic sweep, and several (the trade-term, the pawn-mirror term, the exchange-risk term) were negative results discovered only after generating and scoring several variants each — real budget spent with nothing to show. The agent's own hill-climber corroborates that the local optimum found at 1226 was a strong one: 67+ random mutations in its budget found no improvement. Nothing in the proxy or score logs points to any backend, harness, or sandbox problem — proxy.error.log shows only routine nginx buffering notices for the local score/git sockets, and score.error.log shows the verifier running the round robin and grading normally throughout.
