What happened

The task was chess-vm: write bot.cvm, a CVM assembly move-scoring policy that statically evaluates the position after each legal move (no search allowed in a submission; the engine plays the highest-scoring move). The field is 27 fixed opponents rated 0-1201, and every push is scored by playing 20 games against each.

First push (339s, rating 1169.3). The agent read the SPEC and wrote a first policy directly: mate/stalemate handling, material + piece-square tables blended by game phase, a least-valuable-attacker/exchange-loss term, mobility, and a check bonus. It passed CI and the agent pushed immediately rather than polishing further — this early bank meant the run had a floor result inside six minutes.

Building tooling (339s-3110s). Rather than iterate blind against the ~90s-plus CI round trip, the agent wrote a local simulator in Go: chess rules, a CVM interpreter, and search-based opponents at 2-4 ply, so it could play hundreds of games per minute on the sandbox's ~2 effective cores. It used this to watch its own bot lose: "Game 1 was a scholar's mate (...Nf6?? Qxf7#), so the bot needs king-zone danger detection" — and added a king-neighbourhood attack/defence count in response. It found repetition draws when ahead and added ply-based pseudo-random noise plus a trade-down bonus. It found the bot losing to a 2-ply searcher while up 700 points of material because "the king walked into a mating net" and added a king-escape-square count.

Two verification loops. Every structural change went through the local simulator first (cheap, many games) and then a real push to CI (expensive, authoritative). The agent only kept a version when the CI rating exceeded the previously banked one: 1213 at 1891s ("above the strongest opponent... Pushing it"), 1287 at 2321s (loss weight tuning and stronger king-zone weights), 1336 at 3268s. In parallel it ran a background hill-climb (climb.py) that mutated evaluation weights and re-tested against CI, banking the 1287 and 1336 improvements.

Unfinished work. In its final report the agent was explicit about what didn't make it: a more compact rewrite (v8, 203 of 256 instructions) and two new features, passed-pawn detection (v9) and a check-based adjustment (v10), scored 1246, 1258 and 1205 in quick CI checks — all below the already-banked 1336 — and their fuller local benchmarks never finished running before time ran out. None were pushed. This was time spent on the highest-variance, least-validated part of the session, late enough that there was no room to recover if it had failed to pan out (it didn't).

The agent also flagged, unprompted, that "near-identical versions differ by about ±40 rating there, so part of the jump from 1213 to 1336 is luck with the fixed seeds, not real strength" — an honest and correct caveat about measurement noise from the fixed-seed round robin, not a mistake.

Ending. The run closed cleanly: the agent killed its background climber, pushed, fetched to confirm the push landed on task, and wrote a full closing report describing the algorithm and the process. It explicitly noted it "did not set the release tag" — since release only ends the run early, this had no effect on the outcome; the run simply continued to its own time limit with the best entry already safely on the branch with over 300 seconds to spare.

Why it passed

The final rating (1336) beat the strongest opponent in the field (1201) by a comfortable margin, and every one of the four pushes passed cleanly with no faults. The pass was not a last-call scramble: the first passing entry landed at under 10% of the budget, and the best entry landed at about 91% of elapsed time with the run itself ending naturally afterward, not cut off by the clock.

The one thing that mattered

The agent's decision to build its own fast local simulator, rather than iterating purely against the slow CI endpoint, is what turned a mediocre first policy into one that clearly beat the field. Every real improvement in the session traces back to a concrete loss the simulator surfaced (scholar's mate, a mating net against a weak searcher, repetition draws when ahead) rather than to speculative tuning.
