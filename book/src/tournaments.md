# Tournaments

A tournament is a lobby of seats playing rounds of one game. Any game can be played: a round plays every turn of the game and settles the pairings the way the game says, described under [the pairings](#the-pairings). Seating two of the same agent is allowed, and the standings rate agents, so their pairings against each other count for nothing.

## The lobby

`ava tournament -n <name> -g <game> -t <seconds> -c <combats>` opens a tournament, or the tournaments page of the web interface does. The game, the seconds every run is given, the combats every fight plays, 5 unless chosen otherwise, and the [pairing](#pairing-by-standing), `--pairing round-robin|swiss`, are fixed at that point, and so is the analyst: `--analyst agent[/thinking]` with `--analyst-seconds`, or the analyze checkbox of the form with its seconds field, has every run of a round analyzed the moment it is over, the runs of the seats and the runs of the attacks alike, in parallel with what the round still plays and under the same cap. A failed analysis fails nothing, the run page offers it again. The tournament page shows what was fixed on a card beside the card of its game, the one the games page shows, over the runs of the tournament: the pairing scheme, the seconds of a run and the analyst, with one of the vendored sprite sheets on its cover, a character picked at random whenever the page is rendered and kept for as long as the page stays open. It walks while the tournament is at work, a round in flight with one of its runs up, and stands still otherwise.

A seat holds an agent at a thinking level, on the backend the agent was named with. `-s agent[/thinking]` seats one, repeatable, the agent a name of the registry or `harness/model`, or the seat row on the tournament page does. Two seats may hold the same agent, at different settings or not, which doubles its games a round; the standings rate agents, so the two seats are one entry there. Rounds name seats by number, so a seat that joins stays out of the rounds before it until it is [backfilled](#joining-late), and leaves only until a round holds it, renumbering the seats behind it.

## A round

A round is every seat playing a run of every turn of the game, one turn after the other and all seats at once, then every pairing settled. `ava tournament -n <name>` plays one, and the play round button on the tournament page does. `-j <runs>`, or the field beside the button, caps how many runs a turn has going at once, 8 without it. The cap is what keeps a round of many seats from taking the docker daemon down: every sandbox holds its home and its workspace in memory for as long as it runs. A run not started yet shows as queued in the graph of the round. The runs are ordinary runs: they show up in the runs table like any other, naming the tournament, round and seat they play, and their pages are the run pages. The tournaments table checks tournaments for a [report](reports.md) over them.

The round is written to the record the moment the runs of a turn are named, so the tournament page links the runs while they play. Once every run of a turn is over, the entry of record of each is picked: the entry ranking highest, the newest on ties, which for a pass or fail game is the last passing push. Before the next turn every seat gets what the game asks for in `inputs()`, the entries of record of the other seats from the earlier turns, seeded into its workspace and mounted into its scoring container under the names the game gives them; a seat that kept none leaves that input out, and a run left with nothing to face is not started and wins by forfeit. After the last turn the pairings are settled: every pair of seats in a round robin, the round's pairs in a swiss one. The record is written after every pairing, so a round that breaks off leaves what it had, and only a finished round counts for the standings.

## Pairing by standing

Swiss, the default for a game with a turn facing other seats, pairs each round before it starts: seats rank by the share of their pairings won, draws half, unscored seats last, ties at random, and each meets the highest ranked seat below it not met yet, rematching only when it must. An odd lobby leaves out the lowest ranked of the seats that sat out least. The attack of crackme plays once per pair and direction, each run facing its opponent alone. Rounds record their pairs, so a tournament can switch schemes.

## A round that broke off

A round is over once its pairings are settled and the second it finished is written. A round that lost the process playing it, an interrupt or a restart of the interface included, stays unfinished and out of the standings until it is resumed. Its heading on the tournament page offers three buttons, and the command line the same three ways, each naming the round:

- `continue`, `ava tournament -n <name> --continue <round>`: every run the round lacks a finished one of plays, turn by turn the way a round does, then the pairings are settled and the round is finished. A finished run is kept, whether it banked an entry or not.
- `settle`, `--settle <round>`: nothing plays. What the runs left on disk is banked, the pairings are settled and the round is finished, so a seat that never ran forfeits its pairings.
- `restart`, `--restart <round>`: the runs and the pairings the round holds are dropped and every seat plays it again.

Each of the three banks the entries of the round that have no attempt before anything else happens, read from what their runs left, since a round that broke off after its runs never got to read them. An entry already banked keeps the attempt it was recorded with, which is what the standings have counted.

Only an unfinished round is resumed, and only while nothing else plays. A finished round is over, and playing it again is playing another round.

## Joining late

`ava tournament -n <name> --backfill`, or the backfill button of the tournament page, plays the rounds the seats joined after: in a round robin one run per seat and round, all at once under the cap of a round. The rounds stay unfinished, and out of the standings, until the backfill settles the pairings it adds, and no new round plays while a finished one is missing a seat.

A swiss backfill pairs each missed round up front: its missing seats and the seat it left out meet by their standing then, one left over meets the closest score that played it, and pairs planned for one round count as met in the next. The rounds then play together, turn by turn, only the runs the new pairs need, and keep their finish time.

A round robin of a multi turn game refuses late seats: its later turns face every other seat at once and would have to be played again.

## The pairings

- A pairing the game settles from the records, fib-golf, sanity-check and crackme: nothing is recorded beyond the runs, and the outcome is derived when the standings are shown, so a changed curve changes who won without touching the record. fib-golf and sanity-check compare the points of the two entries of record: more points win the pairing, equal points draw it, as does a game ranking nothing, a missing entry forfeits it. crackme reads the verdicts of the attack turn: two rounds, one per direction, each won by the seat whose keygen cracked the other's crackme, or by forfeit when the other left none.
- A pairing that takes a fight, the r2wars games: the scorer fights the two entries of the last turn over the combats the tournament fixed. Each fight is staged into the scorer image of the game with no network and the entries mounted read only, under a wall clock of 600 seconds for all its combats, and its rounds are tallied from the view of the first seat. The console of the fights is `tournaments/<name>/round-<number>.log`. A seat that left no passing entry forfeits its fights, which are recorded with the reason as the rounds a fight the other seat swept would have tallied, two a combat, so a walkover weighs what a win weighs. A pairing whose fight failed, the wall clock included, is recorded with no rounds and the reason, and counts for nothing.
## The standings

The record holds the tallies of the fights, and the runs and entries everything else is read from. Everything else is derived when the tournament page renders: the matches of the finished rounds between different agents, each scored for the first seat as the rounds won plus half the rounds drawn over the rounds played. An agent is a harness on a model, whatever its settings. The standings list every agent's fights as won-drawn-lost, a fight with more rounds won than lost is won, its score, the rounds won plus half the rounds drawn over the rounds played, the dollars of every run the seat played in the tournament, with what one run of it cost behind the hover, and the largest context a run of the seat reached, with its share of the window behind the hover. The scoreboard rates the agents over those matches, pooled across every tournament. The weights of cost and speed, 0 to 1 each and 0 unless set, move the score of every match before it is rated: the dearer side hands over the cost weight's share of how far apart the two bills are, at the prices of the registry, and the side that banked its entry later the speed weight's share of how far apart the two are in budget spent. A weight of one moves half a match, so the widest gap turns a win into a draw and never into a loss. Every game is weighed the same way, whether its pairings were fought, settled by verdicts or derived from points, and a pairing a side forfeited is not weighed at all. The weights are never stored, the scoreboard takes them from the query. The cross table of every round shows the tally of the row's seat against the column's seat, `forfeit` for a pairing one side did not field an entry for and `none` for one that saw no fight, with the reason behind a tooltip, the cell linking to the run that played the pairing when one did, and a total of the row's fights as won-drawn-lost. A line above it counts the seats that left an entry and what became of the pairings. Two charts follow the standings side by side once a round is finished, drawn as SVG into the page without a script. The score chart has one line per agent, its colour the colour of its avatar, stepping up over the finished rounds by the rounds it won and half the rounds it drew against other agents, so the shape of the tournament reads the way a scoreboard does while a competition runs; hovering a point gives the score and the rounds as won-drawn-lost through that round. The points chart beside it has one line per seat and appears for a game that ranks its entries: the points of the entry of record the seat kept in every finished round, at nothing in a round it kept none. Under the card of every round of such a game a card of its own holds the banked chart: the best points every seat had banked over the seconds of its run, from the start of the scoring clock, every push that raised it a step up, on the points scale of the whole tournament so the rounds compare. A round still playing draws the seats whose runs are over as they end, since a run keeps its entries in its scoring container until then, and the other two charts wait for the round to finish. Hovering a line lights its entry in the legend under the chart and fades the other lines, and hovering an entry does the same for its line. Two seats holding the same agent are one line of the score chart and two of the others, numbered by their seat.
Every round is drawn above its cross table as the graph the tournament walked: a column per turn, a row per seat, every run a node linking its page, and an edge from each entry a run got as its input to that run. A node names the agent seated there, the harness on the model, the run and its state, beside the avatar of the seat. A round still playing fills the top edge of its card with how far it is through the seconds every seat is given. The heading of a round that is over says how long it took.

## Files

- `tournaments/<name>/tournament.json` is the record described in the [data model](data_model.md).
- `tournaments/<name>/round-<number>.log` is what the fights of the round printed.
- `tournaments/<name>/playing` holds the pid of the process playing rounds while it does and the rounds it took, which is how the web interface knows a round the command line plays is going on and which one that is. A backfill holds several rounds unfinished at once, so the rounds the marker names are the ones in flight and every other unfinished round broke off.
- The runs of the rounds are under `runs/`, like every run.
